The port and the adapter exist because `plan.md` puts retention metadata in application configuration
rather than in a table, and because the domain and application layers must not depend on Nextcloud.
The record is stored as one JSON document under one key, so a partial write cannot leave a policy whose
`updatedAt` no longer describes its `retentionYears`.

## The design decision worth recording

`complianceReport()` originally reported `honorsMinimum` from the *effective* policy. That field could
then never be false: `currentPolicy()` substitutes the seven-year default for anything absent or
sub-floor, so the effective policy is compliant by construction. A compliance signal that cannot fail
measures nothing — the worst kind of green, because it reads as evidence.

`honorsMinimum` now describes the **stored** configuration and can be false, which is what surfaces a
discrepancy between what is recorded and what FR-057 requires. A separate `effectiveHonorsMinimum`
states the other fact, that what the service will actually enforce is safe either way. The test that
caught this is `testComplianceReportsASubFloorStoredValueAsNonCompliant`.

## The two permissions, and why the floor refuses rather than clamps

`AuditRetentionPolicy::of()` throws for anything under seven years rather than raising it to seven. A
caller that asked for three years has made a mistake that must be visible; silently substituting a
different period would make the configuration audit record a change the administrator never asked for.

`permitsDeletion()` is not configurable and answers false for every possible policy. FR-057 permits a
policy to govern "archival or availability" and forbids it rewriting business history; Constitution IV
makes audit non-deletable for every role including administrators. Stating the prohibition in the value
object means it does not depend on each caller's judgement.

`configure()` writes the value and then the configuration audit event, in that order, so the recorded
history never claims a change that did not take effect. These are deliberately not one database
transaction: the policy lives in application configuration, not the business database, and a partial
pair would be a policy with no recorded change — not a lost business record.
